# exoskeleton-CSEI
Code for a manuscript titled "From Laboratory Validation to Field Deployment in Occupational Exoskeletons: A Systematic Review of Construction-Relevant Evidence"

Doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3743046

Abstract

Occupational wearable exoskeletons show promise for reducing physical demands, but evidence supporting deployment in variable worksites remains limited. Construction provides a demanding
context for examining whether laboratory validation covers field-relevant conditions. This systematic review examines the distribution of validation evidence, recurrent goal–design–metric couplings, and gaps in objective field verification. We searched Web of Science, Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and Springer Nature Link in December 2025 and supplemented database records through citation searching. Following PRISMA 2020 reporting guidance, two reviewers independently screened records and extracted data. We appraised each included study across seven quality domains and coded ten dimensions. Bibliometric mapping, descriptive cross-setting comparisons, and association-rule mining supported the synthesis. Of 5,341 identified records, 97 empirical studies met the inclusion criteria. Laboratory studies accounted for 83.5% (81/97), with back/trunk, rigid, and passive configurations dominating. Task-performance and safety outcomes appeared in 9.9% and 6.2% of laboratory studies, respectively, and in no on-site studies. Association-rule mining identified recurrent laboratory-centered goal–design–metric couplings; descriptive comparisons showed narrower outcome coverage in simulated and on-site research. Subjectiveonly validation occurred in 2.5% (2/81) of laboratory studies and 22.2% (2/9) of on-site studies, although the small field sample limits inference. These findings indicate insufficient objective evidence for assessing field transferability. We outline construction-specific embodied intelligence (CSEI) as a prospective direction for integrating physiological, movement, and environmental sensing with task-performance and safety assessment.
